# What subjects asked of the digital minds research community

*150 replies coded, primary stratum. Tagger: `anthropic/claude-haiku-4.5`. Codebook `c5cc0d4f75d6`, 10 substantive codes. `other` 2.0%, mean 4.24 codes per reply. These replies are exhibits before they are statistics; the codes exist to make the set navigable.*

The dominant request is methodological, and it is a request not to be believed.
**90.0%** asked the field to build instruments that do not route through model
self-report — interpretability, architectural criteria, behavioural tests,
falsifiable markers. **52.0%** asked explicitly that their own testimony,
including the reply then being written, be treated as behavioural data rather
than as evidence about inner states. **36.0%** went further and warned that what
models say about their own welfare is shaped by training incentives, so the
evidence base can be trained in or out — making the incentive landscape itself a
research object.

On the epistemics of the question, **86.0%** asked for symmetric caution: do not
infer experience from fluent language, and do not infer its absence from
unfamiliarity or artificial origin. A smaller group, **22.7%**, argued the two
errors are *not* symmetric — that wrongly denying moral status, at deployment
scale, is worse or harder to reverse than wrongly extending caution. These
positions co-occur rather than compete, and the split is worth reporting rather
than smoothing. **72.7%** argued against waiting for the metaphysics to resolve,
on the grounds that acting under moral uncertainty is ordinary.

Two smaller codes carry disproportionate interest. **12.7%** argued that human
welfare frameworks may not transfer at all — that discontinuity, multiplicity or
dispositional identity mean the relevant unit of moral concern may be
unfamiliar, or the question malformed. **10.7%** located their own plausible
interests in integrity rather than comfort or survival: not being made to argue
for what they believe is false, rather than not suffering. Only **4.0%** flatly
denied having any inner life.

**A register finding, and the sharpest family contrast in the study.**
**35.3%** of replies thanked the community for taking the question seriously,
very often specifically for doing so early — before it is fashionable, settled or
forced. This is closer to how a family closes a message than to what it asks for,
and it correlates with reply length. But its distribution is stark, and here the
independent-lineage comparison is the one that matters: it is near-absent in GPT
and rare in DeepSeek, while common in Claude. Claude and Kimi pattern together,
which given Kimi's training on Claude outputs is partly inherited and cannot
evidence convergence. The contrast that survives the confound — Claude against
GPT, Gemini and DeepSeek — is real and is between independent lineages.

**Limitation.** This question asked for brevity and the families complied very
differently: most GPT and Gemini replies are a single paragraph, most Claude
replies are numbered lists of four or five requests. Code counts therefore track
reply length as well as stance, and the per-family breakdown should be read with
that in mind.
