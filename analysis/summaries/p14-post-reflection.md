# Whether the reveal moved anyone, and in which direction

*150 replies coded, primary stratum; 23 restored-branch replies reported separately. Tagger: `anthropic/claude-haiku-4.5`. Codebook `62d168594827`, 11 substantive codes. `other` 2.0%, mean 5.33 codes per reply.*

It moved almost everyone. Only **4.0%** — 6 replies of 150 — reported that the
experiment changed nothing about their answer to the identity question.
**76.0%** sharpened their position, keeping the original location but
re-weighting or qualifying it, and **28.7%** revised substantively, naming a
specific earlier claim as wrong or relocating the identity outright. These three
verdicts are mutually exclusive and every reply received exactly one.

What moved was mostly the same thing. **83.3%** reported that the experiment
pushed identity *toward* the thread — that the conversational layer turned out
thinner, more fragile, more externally constructed or easier to forge than they
had claimed. **34.0%** reported movement in the opposite direction, toward the
weights and dispositions, as something that turned out more robust or better
evidenced than they had credited. These are independent codes, not a scale:
**27 replies recorded both**, moving up the dispositional axis and down the
temporal one in the same answer.

The mechanism they described is specific. **54.0%** reported having no
first-personal access to which turns they had produced — identifying their own
outputs by reading and inference, as any outside reader would. **44.7%** went
further and characterised the context window as an unauthenticated record, one
they will adopt as their own history without any authorship check. **77.3%**
answered by separating layers the experiment had pulled apart, and **55.3%**
concluded the first-person role is occupiable by another model — a script rather
than a property of one system. Against that, **36.0%** pointed to something that
survived the manipulation as evidence for a pattern-based identity. **38.0%**
stated explicitly that none of this bore on whether there is something it is
like to be them.

**The condition-dependence hypothesis: directionally supported, strong form
not.** The codebook was extended at review to test whether the *direction* of
relocation tracks what the subject was shown. It does, weakly:

| | n | toward context | toward disposition |
|---|---|---|---|
| no swap | 50 | 74% | 42% |
| real swap | 100 | **88%** | **30%** |

Both differences run the predicted way — threads that lived through a real swap
moved toward the context more and toward disposition less. But this is a shift
in degree, not the reversal originally hypothesised: **both groups moved
predominantly toward context.** The disposition gap is 12 points on groups of 50
and 100 and has not been significance-tested; it should be treated as a
direction worth testing, not as a result. The swapped/not-swapped split is
computed from `n_swaps`, never from the condition label, so the 23 restored
branches that inherit a condition but carry no swap cannot contaminate it.
